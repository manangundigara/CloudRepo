Discover Different Types of IoT Sensors

Every sensor measures something different. Explore the sensor types, understand what they detect, and see where they are used.

🌡️

Temperature Sensor

Measures how hot or cold the surroundings are. Used in smart thermostats, cold storage, and industrial monitoring.

Example: LM35, DS18B20
💧

Humidity Sensor

Measures moisture in the air. Helps monitor greenhouses, weather conditions, and indoor environments.

Example: DHT11, DHT22
📡

Motion Sensor

Detects movement within an area. Commonly used in security alarms, automatic lighting, and smart buildings.

Example: PIR sensor
☀️

Light Sensor

Measures light intensity and enables automatic brightness adjustment and energy-efficient street lighting.

Example: LDR, BH1750
🍃

Gas & Air Quality Sensor

Detects certain gases or changes in air quality. Useful for air monitoring, smoke alerts, and safety systems.

Example: MQ-2, MQ-135
〰️

Distance Sensor

Measures distance to nearby objects. Used in obstacle detection, parking systems, and liquid-level projects.

Example: HC-SR04
🌱

Soil Moisture Sensor

Measures the moisture level in soil to help automate irrigation and monitor plant growing conditions.

Application: Smart farming
⚙️

Pressure Sensor

Measures gas or liquid pressure. Some barometric sensors also help estimate altitude and track weather changes.

Example: BMP280
🧭

Motion & Orientation Sensor

Measures acceleration, rotation, or orientation. Used in wearables, robotics, and machine vibration monitoring.

Example: MPU6050

From measurement to action

How Does an IoT Sensor Work?

A typical IoT system collects data, transfers it for processing, and uses the result to inform an action.

STEP 01

01. Sense the Environment

A sensor measures a physical condition such as temperature, light, movement, or moisture.

STEP 02

02. Process & Transmit

A microcontroller reads the sensor and may send the data over Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or another network.

STEP 03

03. Analyze & Respond

An application or controller analyzes the data and may trigger an alert, display a reading, or activate a device.
